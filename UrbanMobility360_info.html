Notebook 2: 02_data_cleaning.ipynb

ItemDetail
PurposeRemove bad data so we only work with clean, valid records
InputRaw Parquet file + zone lookup
What It Does8-step cleaning pipeline:
  1. Date filter: Keep only January 2024 (removes 2002 dates and Feb leaks)
  2. Duplicates: Remove exact duplicate rows
  3. Distance: Remove trips with 0, negative, or >100 miles
  4. Fare: Remove fares that are 0, negative, or >$500
  5. Passengers: Fill nulls with 1, remove counts >6
  6. Duration: Calculate trip duration, remove <=0 or >3 hours
  7. Zone validation: Remove trips with zone IDs not in lookup
  8. IQR outliers: Remove statistical outliers from distance, fare, duration
OutputSaves yellow_tripdata_2024-01_clean.parquet (2,352,078 rows, 79.3% retention)
Key Finding612,546 bad records removed, keeping 79.3% of data

How We Got The Required Result

Question: WHERE are mobility hotspots?
How: Grouped all trips by pickup zone, counted trips per zone. Also aggregated the Priority Score to zone level. Found: Midtown Center, Upper East Side South, Penn Station area
Question: WHEN is demand highest?
How: Grouped trips by pickup hour. Created peak period classification (Morning Peak 7-9, Evening Peak 17-19). Compared weekday vs weekend. Found: Busiest hours are 5-7 PM
Question: WHY does congestion happen?
How: Compared average speed and congestion rate between peak and off-peak periods. Found: Speed drops 4.6% during peak, congestion rises from 59% to 65%
Question: WHICH routes are inefficient?
How: Calculated Route Efficiency Score = normalized speed (60%) + normalized duration inverse (40%). Found routes with low efficiency. Also found 13 zone-hour combinations classified as High priority
Question: WHAT should be prioritized?
How: Calculated Mobility Priority Score using 4 factors: Demand (30%) + Congestion (30%) + Speed_Inverse (20%) + Duration (20%). Each factor normalized to 0-1 using MinMaxScaler, then weighted and scaled to 0-100. Found: Midtown Center at 5-7 PM is #1 priority (score 64.7, 10,890 trips at 7.8 mph)

⭐ How The Mobility Priority Score Works

Mobility Priority Score = ( 0.30 × Demand_normalized + 0.30 × Congestion_normalized + 0.20 × Speed_Inverse_normalized + 0.20 × Duration_normalized ) × 100

Each Factor Explained

FactorWeightWhat It MeasuresHigher Value Means
Demand30%Number of trips in this zone at this hourMore people affected by problems
Congestion30%Average congestion severity (1-5 scale)More severe traffic congestion
Speed Inverse20%1/speed (lower speed = higher value)Vehicles moving slower
Duration20%Average trip duration in minutesTrips taking longer

Why These Weights?

  • Demand (30%): If nobody uses a route, fixing it has low impact. High demand = high impact.
  • Congestion (30%): Direct measure of the problem we are trying to solve.
  • Speed (20%): Low speed confirms congestion is real, not just high trip count.
  • Duration (20%): Long trip times mean people are wasting more time.

How Normalization Works

Each factor has different units (trips, severity 1-5, mph, minutes). We use MinMaxScaler from Scikit-Learn to convert all to 0-1 range so they can be combined fairly.

Example: If trip counts range from 1 to 10,890 - 10,890 trips → normalized to 1.0 (highest) - 5,445 trips → normalized to 0.5 (middle) - 1 trip → normalized to 0.0 (lowest)

💬 Quick Explanation

"Here is what we did:"

  1. We took 2.9 million NYC taxi trips from January 2024
  2. We cleaned the data — removed bad dates, zero distances, extreme fares, and outliers. Kept 79% of records (2.35 million clean trips)
  3. We added features — calculated speed, identified peak hours, measured congestion severity, mapped zone names
  4. We analyzed the data — found that demand peaks at 5-7 PM, speed drops 4.6% during rush hour, and 65% of peak trips are congested
  5. We created a Mobility Priority Score (0-100) that combines demand, congestion, speed, and duration. This score tells us exactly which areas at which times need the most attention
  6. The highest priority is Midtown Center at 6 PM on weekdays — 10,890 trips crawling at 7.8 mph. This is where the city should focus optimization efforts
  7. We built an interactive Tableau dashboard with 8 sheets and filters so anyone can explore the data by borough, time of day, and peak period

The unique value of this project: Instead of just showing charts, we created a scoring system that ranks every zone-hour combination by how urgently it needs transport optimization. This turns raw data into actionable priorities.
